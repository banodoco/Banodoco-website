02The problem

But its capabilities are scattered across thousands of repos and threads — so few artists, creators and everyday people can use them.

The sprawl of models, nodes, LoRAs and workflows — and how each breakthrough stays with whoever made it.
03The gap

What's missing isn't another model, technical tool or hub. It's an accessible place to create with all of it.

Hubs host the models; node graphs wire them together — both built for engineers. Closed tools are easy but limited. Nothing gives artists the power of open source without the nerd hole.

04The solution

We’re building that place: a new kind of agent-powered creative workspace that unlocks the community’s collective intelligence.

05Astrid

This is Astrid. It starts as a new kind of video editor: describe what you want, and the agent reaches for everything the community has built to make it.

No installs, no node graphs, locally or in the cloud. The agent picks the right models and community-made tools, and when nothing fits, it builds what you need. Every result comes back to your timeline for you to change and build on: you stay the artist. We start with video because that's where open models are moving fastest — and where they're hardest to use.

06Where it goes

As you create, you and your agent build on top of a shared foundation — so the editor grows into a community-built creative platform.

One person's solution becomes someone else's starting point: work out a film-grain look, publish it, and the next artist's agent can reach for it, credited to you. Private projects stay private.

  • Today — a video editor with agents built in, that reshapes itself around each artist.
  • Next — we open it up: anyone can add tools, models and workflows.
  • Then — a platform for every medium, built by everyone who uses it.

07How it pays

Nerds run Astrid locally and build on it; the rest of the world pays for an accessible hosted version.

Builders push Astrid to its limits and publish what they make. That flows into a polished, hosted Astrid for the far larger audience who'd rather not set anything up. They pay for compute and convenience, and a share goes to the makers of the tools they use, which gives builders a reason to keep building. A competitor would have to copy the community, not just the product.

08A thousand flowers

And Astrid isn’t just one app: it’s the technical and inspirational foundation for thousands more, each focused on different niches.

With first-class forking, anyone can spin off their own version to focus the community's output on a particular niche: a style, a medium, an audience. Every fork draws from, and adds to, the same shared pool of tools, so each one makes all the others more valuable.

12The vision

A thriving open-source AI art ecosystem can help accelerate a second renaissance.

Creating with AI stops being one person at a prompt and becomes a collective effort. A painter turns years of her landscapes into a LoRA; elsewhere, an engineer builds a workshop for sculpting in 3D; a year later, in that workshop, a stranger sculpts her paintings into a garden you can walk through. Every finished work, the start of someone else's.
