already wired (e493737) and shells changed (66d1bed), so something regressed

rest; #26 is the leg *leaving* that rest. Doing #26 first would invalidate it.
Strict order. #21 is independent.

## 4. Ordering rationale

- **Verification before construction** (Phase 0): never rebuild something that
  may already be built.
- **Root cause before symptom**: the spore system (Phase 1) sits under two of
  the transition complaints; fixing it first may quieten them and will
  certainly stop later phases from being measured against a moving target.
- **Composition before the leg that leaves it** (Phase 3 before 4): a camera
  leg is defined by its endpoints.
- **Diagnoses before new features**: Phases 1–5 are all "something is wrong";
  Phase 6 is the only genuinely new build. New features on top of unresolved
  faults is how the previous rounds of this project generated repeat reports.
- **Related work batched into one agent run**: 24+22, 19+20, 15+16, 17+18 —
  fewer cold starts, and shared root causes get found rather than papered over
  twice.

## 5. Standing rules for every phase

Unchanged from the project's laws — each agent brief restates them:
- `organism/*` is read-only; steer the particle system through its public API.
- Nothing fades in over open view; reverse scrubs mirror exactly.
- The `mission` reference screenshot stays byte-identical; any other reference
  that legitimately moves is re-shot in the SAME commit with provenance.
- The pre-commit capture gate is never skipped for code.
- No camera roll; rate peaks under ~1.2k deg per unit p.
- Nothing is pushed.

## 6. Known repeat-report risk

Three of these twelve (#22/#24 spores, #25 Connect timing, and the poke half
of #19) are second or third reports of things already worked on. That pattern
means the earlier fixes addressed *a* cause, not *the* cause — or never
reached her build. Every phase touching one of them starts by reproducing the
complaint on the current tree and saying plainly whether it reproduces, before
changing a line.
